Methodology

Interpretation: Scores are provided but prospective students are strongly encouraged to review the actual placements, not the scores or the ranks.

Scoring methodology: A university’s score is the sum of the destination scores for the finance departments that employ its graduates, counting each graduate separately. ASU score, Arizona State University Finance Research Rankings; UTD score, UT Dallas Business School Research Rankings; QS score, QS Accounting & Finance; THE score, Times Higher Education Business and Economics; USN score, U.S. News Economics and Business. Only the top 100 schools (including ties) on each external ranking are scored for that ranking; other destinations have no scored value and contribute zero in that comparison. ASU uses 2016–2025 and UTD uses 2017–2026 (partial 2026), restricted to the Journal of Finance, Journal of Financial Economics, and Review of Financial Studies. Raw scores are not provided here due to potential copyright issues but are publicly available at the time of writing. Top-3s/year at destination is the average of the annualized ASU and UTD article counts (not incomparable authorship weighted scores), using only one if the other is unavailable. Some schools’ tracked placements are almost entirely at the same university. The contribution from these self-placements is capped at 1/4 of the total. This does not affect the top 20 schools in the default 2015+ ranking.

Faculty PhD and employment data: LLMs were used to collect current finance faculty records, initially at schools ranked in the top 50 by at least one source ranking. The dataset covers — faculty and placement-list records and — employing universities and is available online. Official directories, profiles, and CVs were used where available. Selected program placement pages were also scanned for missing graduates and used to cross-check the faculty records. Verified faculty at other universities are also shown, without contributing points. Gaps may reflect unreadable CVs, unverifiable information, errors, or removal requests. Use the removal button below to request deletion. PhD origins are unrestricted. Coverage outside the ranked destination sample is incomplete, and graduates who left academia are generally missed, so these totals are not placement rates. Past placement-list jobs are labeled and shown with a dash. Unverified current jobs and non-finance or unranked destinations receive no points. Each graduate receives credit for one verified current finance-department or university-finance-institute job, choosing the highest destination score when several qualify; duplicate university affiliations count once, and visitor, postdoctoral, teaching-only, courtesy, emeritus, honorary, practice, non-primary, actuarial-science, future, and ended roles are excluded from scoring.

Limitations: All rankings are flawed, especially school rankings. i) Smaller programs have fewer opportunities to earn points; incomplete class records prevent a reliable adjustment. ii) Placements reflect student ability, effort, and luck as well as training. A school offering poor training but attracting excellent students can rank highly. iii) Rankings are imperfect proxies of reality. Your research interests and preferences matter. iv) Economics programs and journals are incompletely covered. Economics-department jobs receive zero points, and publications in economics journals are missed. Input scores may nevertheless include finance publications from economics departments. v) University systems and other cross-university groups are hard to classify. Affiliations follow the documented university; shared programs, campuses, and networks are not automatically merged.

Conflicts of interest: I am Will Gornall, a Stanford GSB graduate, a UBC professor, and the director of UBC’s finance PhD program. I created this page because I thought UBC’s PhD placements were stronger than its faculty research ranking suggested. These affiliations may influence my perspective. I have tried to make reasonable, neutral methodological choices, but those choices involve judgment and can materially affect the results. For example, UBC ranks substantially higher when placements are weighted using Times Higher Education (THE) scores than under the top-3 publication count measure I used as the default. This is an independent project, developed without institutional input from UBC.
